Space and perspective

Depth cues
A picture is a flat surface. It reads as deep because a dozen separate cues all point the same way, and your eye believes the majority.
7 min
Linear perspective
Parallel lines in the world meet at a point in the picture, because equal real distances take up less of your view the further away they are.
8 min
Atmospheric perspective
Distant things go pale, flat and sky-coloured because you are looking at them through more air, and air scatters light.

Light, shadow and value

The single biggest lever over how an image feels.

Light direction
Where the light sits relative to the subject and to the camera. It decides where the shadows land and how much of the shaded side you get to see, which is what makes a flat picture look solid.

Hard and soft light
Whether shadows have crisp edges or fade gradually. It is decided by one thing — how big the light looks from where the subject is standing.

Light falloff

Color

Hue, lightness and chroma — and why they get confused.

Hue, value and chroma
A colour is not one thing. It is three independent dials — which colour it is, how light it is, and how far it sits from grey — and most arguments about colour are two people turning different dials.

The colour wheel, and why there is more than one
A ring of hues in spectral order, closed at the join by colours no wavelength can make. It is a working model, not a picture of light — and different jobs need different wheels, which is why they disagree about what sits opposite what.

The colour of the light

Objects and materials

Why metal, skin, glass and cloth read as themselves.

Diffuse and specular
Light hitting a surface either goes in and comes back out coloured, or bounces straight off keeping the light's own colour. Almost everything a material looks like follows from the split between those two.
9 min
Metal and everything else
Metals have almost no colour of their own and show you the room instead; everything else gets its colour from underneath and reflects the light's own colour on top. This is the sharpest material test you can run on an image.
10 min
Roughness and Fresnel
Two dials that finish a material. Roughness decides how blurry the reflection is; Fresnel makes every surface more reflective at glancing angles. Between them they explain why real objects are never evenly shiny.
